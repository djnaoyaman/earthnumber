These are genuine recorded water reflections, not generated footage. Warm colors are reflections of canyon rock; blue is reflected sky. The visible surface is not a map or a scale model of an exhibit.

## Local derivatives

An 8.5-second silent loop derived from source seconds 8–17; the final half second dissolves to the beginning to soften the seam. Desktop is 768×432 H.264, portrait is a center crop at 420×746. The WebP poster is a still from the same source. No color grading, reversed playback, audio, or third-party soundtrack is added.

The optional interactive WebGL displacement is an illustrative effect added to the recording. It does **not** mean the recorded river responds to input and is **not** an accurate optical or fluid simulation. Scientific diagrams and numbers are outside that visual layer.

The source footage starts downloading only after the visitor explicitly activates the background. Reduced-motion visitors receive the still. If WebGL is unavailable, the ordinary video is used; no synthetic ring fallback is substituted.

Observation reference: Joseph A. Shaw, “Glittering Light on Water,” NOAA PSL, https://psl.noaa.gov/outreach/education/science/glitter/ . This reference informs the observation prompt; no NOAA photographs are redistributed.
